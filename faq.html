This is the working rulebook behind the syndicate concept. Some legal, insurance and operational details will still be finalised in the ownership agreement before anyone commits funds, but this is the structure we are proposing.

What am I actually buying?

A 20% ownership interest in the boat, not a membership or rental allocation. The final ownership agreement will document the legal structure, each share’s rights and the treatment of the syndicate reserve.

How many shares are there?

Five equal ownership shares, each representing 20% of the boat. One share can be held by an individual, couple, family or a small group of mates.

Can several people split one 20% share?

Yes. Internally, that group can split its buy-in and monthly contribution however it likes. For syndicate administration, each 20% share has one nominated representative.

How much is the initial buy-in?

The current working target is about $15,000 per share. At a $65,000 boat purchase price, $13,000 represents the underlying 20% boat interest and roughly $2,000 per share establishes an initial syndicate reserve of about $10,000.

Is the boat already purchased?

No. The current plan is to first assemble the right ownership groups, then proceed subject to a satisfactory water test, independent mechanical inspection, title/registration checks, insurance and the final ownership agreement.

Why does the site mention about 73 days?

One fifth of 365 days is about 73 days. Think of that as the approximate annual usage entitlement or ceiling for each 20% share, not 73 dates blocked out in advance. The calendar stays flexible so unused days are not wasted.

How far ahead can I book?

The standard booking window is three months. That is long enough to organise weekends and trips without allowing one owner to lock up the best dates a year in advance.

How long can one booking be?

Up to seven consecutive days by default. A longer trip can still happen if the other shareholders approve it.

What happens if two shares want the same dates?

Usage is tracked. Where there is a genuine clash, the share with fewer days used gets a slightly earlier booking opportunity. The point is to reward the owner who has had less access, rather than relying on whoever clicks fastest.

What about Christmas, Easter and other popular dates?

They use the same fairness principle. The final agreement can also identify a small number of peak dates for rotation if the group decides that is cleaner. The intention is fair access over time, not permanent ownership of a particular holiday weekend.

Can two or more shareholders use the boat together?

Absolutely, and the system is designed to encourage it. If Share 2 and Share 4 take the boat away together for a four-day long weekend, two usage days are allocated to each share rather than four days to both.

What if three shares go away together?

The same proportional rule applies. A three-day trip shared equally by three ownership groups would ordinarily count as roughly one usage day to each participating share.

Can we use the boat on weekdays or at short notice?

Yes. If the boat is free, the goal is to get it used. The shared calendar is the source of truth and the WhatsApp group can be used to flag spontaneous boating days or invite other shareholders along.

How does the booking system work?

Everyone sees one shared calendar. A booking records the share, dates and useful notes such as pickup/return timing. The calendar controls reservations; the WhatsApp group is for coordination, weather, handovers and last-minute communication.

Where is the boat stored?

The plan is dedicated boat storage in Broadbeach Waters, immediately beside a private boat ramp. This should make launching into the Broadwater much easier than trailering the boat across the Gold Coast for every use.

Does every shareholder need to store the boat at home?

No. The intention is for the boat and trailer to remain at the shared Broadbeach Waters storage location between bookings.

Who pays for fuel?

Fuel is user-pays. The final handover rules will specify the agreed return-fuel standard so the next shareholder is not paying for the previous trip.

What does the $200 per month cover?

Each share contributes $200 per month, creating $12,000 per year across the syndicate. That money is intended for insurance, registration, routine servicing, trailer upkeep, maintenance and gradually maintaining a healthy repair reserve.

Will we definitely spend $12,000 every year?

No. The contribution is a funding target, not a spending target. If normal annual expenses are lower, the surplus remains in the kitty and strengthens the reserve.

Can the $200 monthly contribution change?

Yes. It should be reviewed annually against actual expenses and the reserve balance. If the kitty becomes unnecessarily large, contributions could be reduced or paused. If costs materially increase, shareholders can agree to increase them.

What happens if a major repair is bigger than the kitty?

The reserve is designed to absorb unexpected repairs. If an extraordinary non-insured cost exceeds available funds, the ownership agreement can provide for a special contribution split equally by ownership percentage, subject to an agreed approval process.

Who pays if I damage the boat?

Damage attributable to a particular shareholder or their guests is intended to be that share’s responsibility, including the applicable insurance excess. Ordinary wear, age-related faults and mechanical failures are syndicate expenses.

What happens with normal mechanical failures?

Normal mechanical failure and wear-and-tear are paid from the syndicate kitty, assuming the issue was not caused by misuse or negligence by a specific user.

Will the boat be insured?

Yes. Comprehensive insurance suitable for the final shared-ownership arrangement is a condition of proceeding. The final policy, permitted drivers/operators and excess rules will be confirmed before purchase.

Who organises servicing and maintenance?

The syndicate will nominate one person to coordinate scheduled servicing and maintenance, with costs paid from the kitty. Significant repairs or upgrades should be communicated to all five ownership groups.

What condition must I return the boat in?

Ready for the next shareholder: rubbish removed, interior reasonably clean and dry, equipment returned, any damage/issues reported, and fuel handled according to the agreed policy. Detailed handover rules will be written before launch.

What if I notice a problem during my booking?

Put it in the WhatsApp group immediately and record anything relevant in the booking/handover notes. Safety-critical or potentially damaging issues mean the boat should not keep being operated until checked.

Can we vote on upgrades?

Yes. Routine maintenance is operational. Optional upgrades such as stereo improvements, accessories or major cosmetic work should require an agreed shareholder vote and a clear decision on whether they come from the kitty or a separate contribution.

What if I want to sell my share?

The intended model is to give existing shareholders first opportunity to buy the departing share at fair market value before an approved replacement shareholder is introduced. The final agreement will set notice periods, valuation and approval mechanics.

Do I get any of the kitty back when I sell?

The intention is that a share represents its proportional interest in the syndicate’s net reserve as well as the boat, after allowing for liabilities and committed expenses. The exact mechanism will be documented in the final agreement.

What happens if the whole boat is sold?

Sale proceeds would be applied to outstanding syndicate liabilities first, then the remaining net proceeds and reserve distributed according to ownership percentages, subject to the final agreement.

What happens if a shareholder stops paying the monthly contribution?

The ownership agreement needs a clear default process, including notice, a period to catch up and ultimately a mechanism to recover arrears or force a transfer/sale of the share if payments remain unpaid.

Can anyone drive or operate the boat?

Only people permitted under the final insurance policy and applicable Queensland licensing rules should operate it. Each share is responsible for making sure its nominated operators comply.

How many people can the VTX carry?

The 2008 Malibu Wakesetter VTX is rated for up to 10 people. In practice, fewer passengers will be more comfortable once bags, boards, eskies and other gear are aboard.

What do we know about this particular boat?

The current local listing is a 2008 Malibu Wakesetter VTX advertised with about 210 hours. The seller says they are only selling to upgrade. The boat and trailer are registered, it has been fully detailed inside and out, and comes with a full cover, new battery, ballast tanks, Power Wedge, board racks and a fold-down tower. Those seller-provided details still need to be independently verified before purchase.

What if the inspection finds problems?

The syndicate does not have to proceed. The inspection can be used to renegotiate the purchase price, require repairs, or walk away and find another suitable boat.

Is this a commercial boat-share business?

No. The current concept is a private shared-ownership arrangement between five ownership groups. The final legal, insurance and accounting structure will be documented before money is committed.

How will the five shareholders communicate?

A shared WhatsApp group for day-to-day communication plus the shared booking calendar. Important rules, ownership decisions and financial records should also be kept in a permanent shared record rather than relying only on chat history.
